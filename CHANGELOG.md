## 0.2.7 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/RegularLocalization.lean`: let $R$ be a regular local ring and let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal. Then $R_\mathfrak{p}$ is a regular local ring (`isRegularLocalRing_localization`). The proof shows $\operatorname{pd} \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) < \infty$ over $R_\mathfrak{p}$ with the module $(R/\mathfrak{p})_\mathfrak{p} \cong \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$. The file also states the characterization of Serre in both directions (`isRegularLocalRing_iff_exists_hasProjectiveDimensionLT`).
- `Singularity/Algebra/SerreConverse.lean`: the converse of the theorem of Serre. Let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring with residue field $k$. If $\operatorname{pd} k < \infty$, then $R$ is regular (`isRegularLocalRing_of_hasProjectiveDimensionLT`). The proof uses prime avoidance with the associated primes, the change of rings $\operatorname{pd}_{R/(x)} (M/xM) \le \operatorname{pd}_R M$ for a nonzerodivisor $x$ on $R$ and $M$, and the fact that $k$ is a direct summand of $\mathfrak{m}/x\mathfrak{m}$ for $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$.
- `Singularity/Algebra/SerreConverse.lean`: let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring. If some $s \ne 0$ has $\mathfrak{m} s = 0$, then each finite module of finite projective dimension is free (`free_of_hasProjectiveDimensionLT_of_socle`). If also $\operatorname{pd} k < \infty$, then $\mathfrak{m} = 0$ (`maximalIdeal_eq_bot_of_socle`). This is the depth zero step of the converse of the theorem of Serre.
- `Singularity/Algebra/GlobalDimension.lean`: over a regular local ring $R$ of dimension $d$, each finite module has projective dimension at most $d$ (`projectiveDimension_le_ringKrullDim`). The proof is by induction on $d$ with the quotient $R/(x)$ for $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$. The file also proves a change of rings: if $x$ is a nonzerodivisor and $N$ is a finite $R/(x)$-module, then $\operatorname{pd}_R N \le \operatorname{pd}_{R/(x)} N + 1$ (`hasProjectiveDimensionLT_of_quotient`).
- `Singularity/Algebra/ExtCriterion.lean`: let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring with residue field $k$, and let $M$ be a finite $R$-module. Then $\operatorname{pd} M \le n$ if and only if $\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, k) = 0$ (`projectiveDimension_le_iff_subsingleton_ext`). The case $n = 0$ is the statement that $M$ is free if $\operatorname{Ext}^1(M, k) = 0$. Mathlib has the criterion with all finite test modules. This file needs only the test module $k$.
- The workflow `trust` has a new job `lean4lean`. It checks each declaration of the package Singularity again with lean4lean, a type checker for Lean written in Lean. The job checks each module and takes its imports as correct, so it does not check Mathlib again. lean4lean follows the design of the C++ kernel, so this is a second implementation but not an independent design.

## 0.2.6 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/HilbertSamuel.lean`: the Hilbert-Samuel function `hilbertSamuel I M n`, the length of $M/I^n M$ in $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$. The file proves its value at 0, the recursion $\chi(n+1) = \chi(n) + \ell(I^n M/I^{n+1} M)$, the monotony and the comparison of two ideals. If $R$ is Noetherian, $M$ is finite and $I$ contains a power of a maximal ideal, then each value is finite (`hilbertSamuel_ne_top`). The file also defines the order `order I f` of an element and proves its laws: the order of a sum and of a product, and the intersection theorem of Krull in the form "only 0 has order infinity" for a Noetherian local ring.
- `Singularity/Algebra/RegularSequence.lean`: the maximal ideal of a regular local ring of dimension $d$ is generated by a regular sequence of length $d$ (`exists_isRegular_ofList_eq_maximalIdeal`). With the Mathlib theorem on the quotient by a regular sequence, the residue field has projective dimension $d$ (`projectiveDimension_residueField`). This is the first half of the theorem of Serre.

## 0.2.5 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Scheme/Components.lean`: facts about the irreducible components of a reduced Noetherian scheme $X$. Each component, with its subscheme structure, is reduced and integral. A point whose local ring is a domain lies on one component only. Over the complement of the other components, the inclusion of a component is an isomorphism.
- `Singularity/Scheme/Reduction.lean`: `reductionToIntegral`, the proof of `ReductionToIntegral`. Let $X$ be a variety over $k$ with irreducible components $X_1, \dots, X_r$. Resolve each $X_i$, and let $Y$ be the disjoint union of the resolutions. Then $Y \to X$ is a resolution of $X$. The file also proves that a finite disjoint union of proper maps to one scheme is proper, with the valuative criterion. The theorem uses only the standard axioms.
- `Singularity/Curves/Resolution.lean`: `resolutionInDim_one`. Each variety of dimension at most 1 over a field of any characteristic has a resolution. The theorem takes no hypothesis.
- `base/SingularityBase/CharZero.lean`: `resolutionInDim_charZero`. Each variety over a field of characteristic 0 has a resolution, with no hypothesis.

### Changed

- `ReductionToIntegral` is no longer an open hypothesis. The theorems that take it as an argument stay, and the new theorems apply them to `reductionToIntegral`.

## 0.2.4 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/NormalLocus.lean`: let $A$ be a domain with a finite normalization $\tilde A$, and let $p$ be a prime ideal of $A$ such that $A_p$ is integrally closed. Then there is an element $s \notin p$ with $s \tilde A \subseteq A$, and $A_s$ is integrally closed. Thus the normal locus of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is open.
- `Singularity/Curves/Resolution.lean`: `integralResolutionInDim_one`. Let $X$ be an integral variety of dimension at most 1 over a field of any characteristic. Then the normalization $Y \to X$ is a resolution of singularities of $X$. The map is finite, $Y$ is regular, the regular locus of $X$ is open, and the map is an isomorphism over it. The theorem uses only the standard axioms. The corollary `resolutionInDim_one_of_reductionToIntegral` gives `ResolutionInDim p 1` under the named hypothesis `ReductionToIntegral`.

## 0.2.3 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/NormalizationCharP.lean`: `finite_integralClosure`. Let $A$ be a domain that is a finitely generated algebra over a field $k$ of any characteristic. Then the integral closure of $A$ in $\operatorname{Frac} A$ is a finite $A$-module. This is the theorem of E. Noether. The file proves the purely inseparable case over $k[X_1, \dots, X_s]$ with the ring $k'[Y_1, \dots, Y_s]$, where $Y_i^q = X_i$ and $k'$ is a finite extension of $k$. Then it reduces the general case to this case and to the separable case with the normal closure.
- `Singularity/Curves/Affine.lean`: `isDedekindDomain_integralClosure` and `isRegularRing_integralClosure`. The normalization of an affine curve over a field of any characteristic is a Dedekind domain, so it is a regular ring.

### Changed

- `Singularity/Algebra/Normalization.lean`: the new lemma `finite_integralClosure_of_finite_of_finite` has the step from the integral closure of $P$ to the integral closure of $A$. The lemma `finite_integralClosure_of_finite` now uses it.
- `Singularity/Curves/Affine.lean`: the characteristic 0 theorems are now corollaries of the general theorems.

## 0.2.2 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Curves/Affine.lean`: `isDedekindDomain_integralClosure_of_charZero` and `isRegularRing_integralClosure_of_charZero`. Let $A$ be a domain of dimension at most 1 that is a finitely generated algebra over a field of characteristic 0. Then the normalization of $A$ is a Dedekind domain, and so its localization at each prime ideal is a regular local ring. This is the affine part of the resolution of curves in characteristic 0.
- `Singularity/Scheme/NormalDimOne.lean`: `isRegularScheme_of_isIntegrallyClosed_stalk`. Let $X$ be a locally Noetherian scheme of dimension at most 1. If each local ring of $X$ is an integrally closed domain, then $X$ is regular. Mathlib has no class for normal schemes, so the hypothesis is written on the local rings.
- `Singularity/Algebra/Normalization.lean`: `finite_integralClosure_of_finite`. Let $P \to A$ be a finite injective map of domains, with $P$ Noetherian and integrally closed, and with $\operatorname{Frac} A$ separable over $\operatorname{Frac} P$. Then the integral closure of $A$ is a finite $A$-module. The corollary `finite_integralClosure_of_charZero` gives the finiteness of the normalization of each finitely generated domain over a field of characteristic 0.

## 0.2.1 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/Regular.lean`: the first facts about regular local rings. If $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$, then $R/(x)$ is regular of dimension $\dim R - 1$. A regular local ring is a domain. A Noetherian local domain of dimension 1 is regular if and only if it is a discrete valuation ring, and if and only if it is integrally closed. Sections 1 to 4 are adapted from resolutionorg/hironaka with attribution.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-11-23

### Added

- The workflow `trust`. Each week, and on request, it replays each declaration of `Singularity` and of `SingularityBase` in a fresh environment with `leanchecker` of the toolchain. This is a part of check Q3. `leanchecker` uses the kernel of Lean itself, thus it is not an independent kernel.
- `Singularity/Hypotheses/Integral.lean`: `IntegralResolutionInDim p n`, the main statement for integral varieties, and the named hypothesis `ReductionToIntegral`, the step from integral varieties to reduced varieties. The hypothesis is a `def ... : Prop`, not an axiom.
- The package `base/` (`SingularityBase`). It depends on resolutionorg/hironaka at the pin in `manifests/base.yaml` and proves `Singularity.Base.integralResolutionInDim_zero`: each integral variety over a field of characteristic 0 has a resolution. The proof applies Main Theorem I of the base and proves that the exceptional divisor of the blowup has a dense complement.
- `Singularity.resolutionInDim_zero`: resolution in characteristic 0 in each dimension, under the hypothesis `ReductionToIntegral`.
- `Singularity/Bridge/FormalConjectures.lean`: a copy of the definition `Scheme.HasResolution` of google-deepmind/formal-conjectures, and its statements `FCResolutionInDim p n` and `FCResolutionCharZero` as propositions.
- `base/SingularityBase/Bridge.lean`: `hasResolution_of_isResolution`, a resolution of an integral variety over a perfect field in the sense of the project is a resolution in the sense of formal-conjectures. Also `fcResolutionInDim_of_resolutionInDim` and `fcResolutionCharZero`, the statement `resolution_of_singularities_of_charZero` of formal-conjectures with no named hypothesis.
- The workflow `base`, which builds the base package and keeps the build of resolutionorg/hironaka in an actions cache.

### Changed

- `scripts/Export.lean` accepts the root module as an optional fourth argument.
- `scripts/scan.sh` also scans `base/`.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-11-23

### Added

- The statements of the problem: `regularLocus`, `IsRegularScheme`, `IsResolution`, `IsVariety`, `ResolutionInDim`, `ResolutionOfSingularities`, `ResolutionDimFourCharP` and `PurelyInseparableHypersurfaceResolution`.
- Fidelity check F2: `isResolution_id`, the identity of a regular scheme is a resolution.
- Fidelity check F3: `not_isResolution_codiag`, the codiagonal $X \sqcup X \to X$ is not a resolution when $X$ has a regular point.
- Fidelity of the core problem: `purelyInseparableHypersurfaceResolution_of_resolutionInDim`, P1 is a special case of `ResolutionInDim p n` for $p \neq 0$. The proof shows that the hypersurface $z^p + g = 0$ has dimension at most $n$ and is a variety when it is reduced.
- `resolutionInDim_dim_zero`: each variety of dimension at most 0 has a resolution, in each characteristic. A reduced scheme of dimension at most 0 is regular, so the identity is a resolution.
- `scripts/Export.lean`, which writes the record of the build, and `scripts/axioms.py`, which checks the axioms in the record.
- `scripts/scan.sh`, which finds `sorry`, `admit`, `axiom` and `native_decide` in the main tree.
- The workflows `build`, `check` and `scorecard`.
- `formalization.yaml`, the metadata of the formalization, and `manifests/statements.sha256`, the hashes of the statement files.
