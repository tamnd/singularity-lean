# Changelog

All changes that a user can see are in this file. The format follows Keep a Changelog, and the versions follow Semantic Versioning.

## Unreleased

### Added

- `Singularity/Valuation/Augmentation.lean`: parts 1 and 2 of Theorem 3.3.5 of MacLane-Vaquié (Section 3.3 of Volume 3 of the handbook). Let `φ` be a key polynomial for a valuation `μ` on `K[X]`, and let `γ ≤ μ φ` in multiplicative notation. Then the augmentation `[μ; φ, γ]` is a valuation (`augmentedValuation`, `KeyPolynomial.augmentedValuationFun_mul`). It satisfies `μ ≤ [μ; φ, γ]` in additive notation (`augmentedValuation_le`), it agrees with `μ` in degree less than the degree of `φ`, and it sends `φ` to `γ`. If `μ` has support `0`, then the support is `0` for `γ ≠ 0` and `(φ)` for `γ = 0` (`augmentedValuation_eq_zero_iff`, `augmentedValuation_zero_eq_zero_iff`). The file also proves that `φ` is `μ`-minimal if and only if `μ` is equal to `[μ; φ, μ φ]` (`isMuMinimal_iff`). Parts 3 and 4 of Theorem 3.3.5 are not proved.

## 0.4.3 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Valuation/KeyPolynomials.lean`: the key polynomials of MacLane (Section 3.3 of Volume 3 of the handbook, node SG0000000Z). Let `μ` be a valuation on `K[X]`. The file defines `μ`-equivalence (`MuEquiv`), `μ`-divisibility (`MuDvd`) and the key polynomials (`KeyPolynomial`, Definition 3.3.3). The conditions on initial forms in the graded algebra are stated with polynomials, and the docstring of the file explains why the two forms agree. A key polynomial has positive degree (`KeyPolynomial.natDegree_pos`) and is not a unit. The file proves Lemma 3.3.2: the monomial valuation `[v; X, γ]` is a valuation (`monomialValuation`, `monomialValuationFun_mul`), with support `0` if `γ ≠ 0`. The polynomial `X` is a key polynomial for it (`keyPolynomial_X_monomialValuation`). The file defines the coefficients of the `φ`-expansion (`expansionCoeff`, `eq_sum_expansionCoeff`) and the augmented valuation `[μ; φ, γ]` as a function (`augmentedValuationFun`, Definition 3.3.4). It agrees with `μ` in degree less than the degree of `φ`, it sends `φ` to `γ`, and it satisfies the ultrametric inequality. The file does not prove Theorem 3.3.5 of MacLane-Vaquié.

## 0.4.2 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Valuation/Abhyankar.lean`: the inequality of Abhyankar (Theorem 2.1.5 of Volume 3 of the handbook, node SG0000000Y). Let `A` be a valuation subring of a field `K` that is an algebra over a field `k`. Then the rank over `ℚ` of `ℚ ⊗[ℤ] Γ` plus the transcendence degree of the residue field of `A` over `k` is at most the transcendence degree of `K` over `k`, where `Γ` is the value group of `A` (`abhyankar_inequality`). The proof is the monomial argument (`algebraicIndependent_sumElim_of_monoidHom`): units with values that are independent over `ℤ`, together with elements whose residues are algebraically independent, are algebraically independent (`algebraicIndependent_sumElim_of_valuationSubring`). The file also proves Lemma 2.1.2 (`exists_valuation_aeval_eq`) and Theorem 2.1.4 (`algebraicIndependent_sumElim_of_hasExtension`) for an extension of valued fields, and the relative inequality (`abhyankar_inequality_relative`). In the relative inequality, the value group of `L` modulo the value group of `K` is the image of the value group of `vL` in the quotient of `Γ₁ˣ` by the value group of the restriction of `vL` to `K`.

- `Singularity/Valuation/ZariskiRiemann.lean`: the Zariski-Riemann space and its quasi-compactness (Theorem 1.5.3 of Volume 3 of the handbook, node SG0000000X). Let `K` be a field and let `A` be a commutative ring with an algebra map `A → K`. The space `ZariskiRiemann A K` is the set of valuation subrings of `K` that contain the image of `A`. Its topology is generated by the sets `basicOpen F` of the valuation subrings that contain a finite set `F`. The file proves that these sets are open and are a basis of the topology (`isTopologicalBasis_basicOpen`), that the space is not empty, and that it is quasi-compact (`ZariskiRiemann.compactSpace`). The proof takes the limit of an ultrafilter: the elements `x` such that the set of the points that contain `x` is in the ultrafilter make a valuation subring.


## 0.4.1 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Statements/Embedded.lean`: the statement of the embedded resolution of surfaces of Cossart, Jannsen and Saito [CJS20] (Theorem 3.1.4 of Volume 4 of the handbook, node SG0000001C). `EmbeddedResolutionSurface` is a definition of a proposition, and the package does not prove it. It asks for a regular scheme `Z` of finite type over a field and a reduced closed subscheme `X` of dimension at most 2. Then there is a proper morphism `π : Z' ⟶ Z` with `Z'` regular, an open `U` that contains each point where `X` is regular, and an ideal sheaf `E` on `Z'` such that `π` is an isomorphism over `U`, the support of `E` is the preimage of the complement of `U`, `E` has simple normal crossings, and the strict transform of `X` has normal crossings with `E` (`IsEmbeddedResolution`). Mathlib has no excellent schemes and no blowups of schemes, so the statement does not ask for an excellent `Z` or for a sequence of blowups in permissible centers. The file defines the vocabulary: a regular system of parameters (`IsRegularSystemOfParameters`), the stalk of an ideal sheaf (`stalkIdeal`), a divisor with simple normal crossings (`IsSncDivisor`, node SG0000000V, Stacks tag 0BI9), normal crossings of a subscheme with a divisor (`HasNormalCrossings`), normal flatness (`IsNormallyFlatAt`), a permissible center (`IsPermissible`, node SG0000000T), a regular subscheme of codimension 1 (`IsRegularCodimOne`) and the strict transform (`strictTransform`).
- `Singularity/Fidelity/Embedded.lean`: the fidelity checks of the statement. The identity of a regular scheme is an embedded resolution of the empty subscheme (`isEmbeddedResolution_id_top`). On a nonempty regular scheme the zero ideal sheaf is not a divisor with simple normal crossings (`not_isSncDivisor_bot`).
- `Singularity/Scheme/Embedded.lean`: a closed point is a permissible center (`isPermissible_vanishingIdeal_singleton`, node SG0000000T), because each module over a field is flat. A regular closed subscheme of codimension 1 in a regular scheme is a divisor with simple normal crossings (`isSncDivisor_of_codim_one`, node SG0000000V), because it is defined at each point by one member of a regular system of parameters ([Mat89, Theorem 14.2]). The identity is an embedded resolution of such a subscheme when it is reduced (`isEmbeddedResolution_id_of_codim_one`).

## 0.3.2 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/CurveOnSurface.lean`: the blowup of a regular curve on a regular surface. Let $(R, \mathfrak m)$ be a regular local ring and let $P$ be an ideal such that $R/P$ is a regular local ring with $\dim R/P + 1 = \dim R$. Then $P = (x)$ for an element $x \in \mathfrak m \setminus \mathfrak m^2$ (`exists_eq_span_singleton_of_isRegularLocalRing_quotient`, [Mat89, Theorem 14.2], Stacks tag 00NQ). The case $\dim R = 2$, $\dim R/P = 1$ is `exists_eq_span_singleton_of_ringKrullDim_eq_two`. If $I \subseteq \mathfrak m^2$, then $\mathfrak m$ needs at most as many generators as the maximal ideal of $R/I$ (`spanFinrank_maximalIdeal_le_of_le_sq`). The chart $R[I/x]$ of the blowup along an ideal $I$ is the $R$-subalgebra of $R_x$ generated by the fractions $a/x$ with $a \in I$ (`idealBlowupChart`), and `blowupChart R x` is the case $I = \mathfrak m$ (`blowupChart_eq_idealBlowupChart`). If $I = (x)$ with $x$ a nonzerodivisor, then $R \to R[I/x]$ is an isomorphism (`idealBlowupChartEquivOfEqSpanSingleton`). So the blowup of $R$ along $P$ is the identity on the chart $R[P/x]$, and this chart covers the blowup (`bijective_algebraMap_idealBlowupChart_of_isRegularLocalRing_quotient`, `bijective_algebraMap_idealBlowupChart_of_ringKrullDim_eq_two`). The file does not formalize `Proj`.

## 0.3.1 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Invariant/Directrix.lean`: the points where the order stays the same under a point blowup lie in the directrix [Hir67]. The directrix of $g$ is the subspace of the vectors $v$ with $g(x + s v) = g(x)$ in $k[x, s]$ (`directrix`). If $v$ is in the directrix, then $g(x + c v) = g(x)$ for each $c \in k$ (`aeval_add_smul_of_mem_directrix`). Let $\operatorname{ord} f = m$, and let the $x_j$-chart with translations $t$ send $f$ to $x_j^m f'$. If $\operatorname{ord} f' = m$, then the vector of the chart lies in the directrix of the part $f_m$ of $f$ of degree $m$ (`mem_directrix_of_ord_pointChart_eq`). The field can have any characteristic.

## 0.3.0 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Invariant/OrderBlowup.lean`: the order does not go up under a point blowup [Hau03]. If $m \le \operatorname{ord} f$, the $x_j$-chart sends $f$ to $x_j^m f'$ (`exists_pointChart_eq_X_pow_mul`). If $\operatorname{ord} f = m$, then $\operatorname{ord} f' \le m$ (`ord_le_of_pointChart`). The chart is injective (`pointChart_injective`), so $f' \ne 0$ when $f \ne 0$ (`ne_zero_of_pointChart_eq_X_pow_mul`).
- `Singularity/Algebra/BlowupChart.lean`: the charts of the blowup of a regular local ring are regular. Let $R$ be a regular local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ and residue field $\kappa$, and let $x \in \mathfrak m \setminus \mathfrak m^2$. The chart $R[\mathfrak m/x]$ is the $R$-subalgebra of $R_x$ generated by the fractions $a/x$ with $a \in \mathfrak m$ (`blowupChart`). It is a regular ring (`isRegularRing_blowupChart`), and it is Noetherian (`isNoetherianRing_blowupChart`). Let $x, y_1, \dots, y_d$ generate $\mathfrak m$ with $\dim R = d + 1$. Then $R[\mathfrak m/x]/(x)$ is isomorphic to $\kappa[T_1, \dots, T_d]$ (`blowupChartQuotientEquiv`). The proof uses the fact that the associated graded ring of $R$ is a polynomial ring over $\kappa$ (`coeff_mem_maximalIdeal_of_aeval_mem_pow`, [Mat89, Theorem 14.4]).

## 0.2.15 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Example/HauserPerlegaCycle.lean`: one full cycle of the first example of Hauser and Perlega [HP19a, Section 4] (`hauserPerlegaCycle`). The instance has characteristic 2, $n = 5$, $q = 8$, $d = 2$, $a = b = r = 0$, $s = 1$, $\lambda = 1$, $A = 1$ and $Q = 0$. The file certifies the 7 steps of the cycle (20 point blowups). At each blowup the total transform is $x_j^8 F'$ with $\operatorname{ord} F' \ge 8$. The residual order along the divisor is 2 at the start, 6 after step (1), 5 after steps (2) to (5), and 4 after step (6) and during step (7). The final equation has the shape of the start with $d + 2$ and with $y$ and $u$ exchanged.
- `Singularity/Example/Narasimhan.lean`: the example of Narasimhan [Nar83] in characteristic 2 (`narasimhan`, Example 2.2.1 of Volume 9 of the handbook). Let $f = x^2 + y z^3 + z w^3 + y^7 w$ and $\gamma(t) = (t^{32}, t^7, t^{19}, t^{15})$. Then $f$ and all its partial derivatives vanish on $\gamma$ (`aeval_narasimhanCurve_narasimhanF`, `aeval_narasimhanCurve_pderiv`). Each formal power series $h$ in $K[[x, y, z, w]]$ with $h(\gamma(t)) = 0$ has order at least 2 (`two_le_order_of_subst_eq_zero`). The substitution is `MvPowerSeries.subst`. So no smooth formal hypersurface germ at the origin contains the points of order 2 of $f$ (`subst_narasimhanCurve_ne_zero`), and a hypersurface of maximal contact does not exist in this case.

## 0.2.14 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Invariant/Order.lean`: the order `ord f` of a polynomial at the origin, the smallest total degree of a monomial of `f`, defined through `MvPowerSeries.order`. The file proves that $\operatorname{ord}(fg) = \operatorname{ord} f + \operatorname{ord} g$ over a domain (`ord_mul`), that the order of a sum is at least the smaller order (`min_ord_le_ord_add`), and that $\operatorname{ord} f \ge n$ if and only if $f \in \mathfrak{m}^n$ (`le_ord_iff_mem_pow`). It defines the order of an ideal (`idealOrd`) and proves that the order of a product of ideals is the sum (`idealOrd_mul`). The order does not change under an invertible linear change of coordinates (`ord_linearChange`). It agrees with the order `Singularity.Algebra.order` for the ideal of the variables (`ord_eq_order_idealOfVars`).
- `Singularity/Invariant/Newton.lean`: the Newton polyhedron `newtonPolyhedron f`, the convex hull of the exponents of `f` plus the positive orthant. The polyhedron of a product is in the Minkowski sum of the polyhedra (`newtonPolyhedron_mul_subset`). The file defines weighted orders and initial forms, and proves that the initial form of a product is the product of the initial forms over a domain (`initialForm_mul`). The order is the least total degree on the polyhedron (`isLeast_sum_newtonPolyhedron`).
- `Singularity/Invariant/Residual.lean`: cleaning and the residual order of a purely inseparable equation $z^q + F$. `residualOrder q F` is the largest order of $F + h^q$, and in characteristic $p$ with $q = p^e$ it is the order of the clean part of $F$ over a perfect field (`residualOrder_eq_ord_cleanPart`). `residualOrderAlong` is the residual order along a normal crossings divisor [HP19a, §2]. The file defines the charts of a point blowup and the weak transform (`pointChartZ_purelyInseparable`). The bound of Moh on the increase of the residual order under one blowup is stated as the proposition `moh_bound`, and it is not proved.
- `Singularity/Example/HauserPerlega.lean`: the example of Hauser and Perlega [HP19a, §3] in characteristic 2 (`kangaroo`). The residual order of $z^4 + x^2 y^2 w^3 (w (x+y)^4 + x^{13})$ is 5, and after a permissible point blowup it is 7. The increase is 2, so the bound of Moh holds with equality, and the bound 6 from the proof of the stability theorem of Moh fails (`kangaroo_increase`).
- `Singularity/Algebra/FlatDescent.lean`: flat descent of regularity. Let $A \to B$ be a flat local homomorphism of local rings with $A$ Noetherian. If $B$ is regular, then $A$ is regular (`isRegularLocalRing_of_flat`, [Mat89, Theorem 23.7 (i)]).
- `Singularity/Algebra/RegularLocusField.lean`: the regular locus of an algebra of finite type over an arbitrary field $k$ is open (`isOpen_regularLocus_of_field`). The field may be imperfect. Generic regularity goes down along a finite injective map of Noetherian domains (`exists_ne_zero_basicOpen_subset_regularLocus_of_finite`). It holds when the fraction field is separably generated over $k$ (`exists_ne_zero_basicOpen_subset_regularLocus_of_isSeparablyGenerated`). It holds for every domain of finite type over $k$ (`exists_ne_zero_basicOpen_subset_regularLocus_of_finiteType`), because a finite extension of $k$ reduces it to the separably generated case.
- `Singularity/Scheme/RegularLocus.lean`: the regular locus of a scheme that is locally of finite type over a field is open (`regularLocus_isOpen_of_field`).

## 0.2.11 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/FiniteDifference.lean`: a function $f : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{Z}$ is eventually polynomial of degree less than $d$ (`IsEventuallyPolynomial f d`) if it agrees for large $n$ with a polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[X]$ of degree less than $d$. The file proves that this holds if and only if $\Delta^d f$ is eventually $0$, and it proves the laws for sums, partial sums and shifts. It also defines the eventual value of a function that is eventually constant.
- `Singularity/Algebra/HilbertSerre.lean`: the theorem of Hilbert and Serre (`isEventuallyPolynomial_length_of_grading`). Let $N = \bigoplus_n N_n$ be a Noetherian module over an $R$-algebra generated by $r$ elements that raise the degree by $1$, with each $N_n$ of finite length. Then $n \mapsto \ell(N_n)$ is eventually a polynomial of degree less than $r$. The proof is by induction on $r$ with the kernel and the cokernel of the last generator [AM69, Theorem 11.1]. The file applies the theorem to the associated graded module of $M$ for $I$. Thus, if $R$ is Noetherian, $M$ is finite, $I$ has $r$ generators and $M/IM$ has finite length, the Hilbert-Samuel function is eventually a polynomial of degree at most $r$ (`exists_hilbertSamuel_polynomial`).
- `Singularity/Algebra/Multiplicity.lean`: the Samuel multiplicity `samuelMultiplicity I M d`, the eventual value of $\Delta^d$ of the Hilbert-Samuel function. It is $d!$ times the coefficient of $n^d$ of the polynomial (`samuelMultiplicity_eq_coeff`), it is $0$ above the degree, and it is positive at the degree when $R$ is local, $I$ is in the maximal ideal and $M \neq 0$ (`samuelMultiplicity_pos`). A larger ideal gives a smaller multiplicity (`samuelMultiplicity_anti`). A field and a discrete valuation ring have multiplicity $1$ (`samuelMultiplicity_field`, `samuelMultiplicity_dvr`). The value $1$ for each regular local ring is not yet proved.
- `Singularity/Algebra/MultiplicityRegular.lean`: let $R$ be a regular local ring of dimension $d$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Then $\ell(R/\mathfrak m^{n+1}) = \binom{n+d}{d}$ for each $n$ (`hilbertSamuel_succ_eq_choose`), $\ell(\mathfrak m^n/\mathfrak m^{n+1}) = \binom{n+d-1}{d-1}$ for $d \geq 1$ (`length_grPiece_eq_choose`), and the multiplicity $e_d(\mathfrak m, R)$ is $1$ (`samuelMultiplicity_regular`). The proof does not use the associated graded ring. The main step is $(\mathfrak m^{n+1} : a) = \mathfrak m^n$ for the first element $a$ of a regular sequence that generates $\mathfrak m$. It comes from a general fact: if $I$ is generated by a weakly regular sequence in the maximal ideal of a Noetherian local ring and $z$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R/I$, then $z$ is a nonzerodivisor on each $R/I^n$ (`mem_pow_of_mul_mem_of_isWeaklyRegular`). The result holds for each Noetherian local ring whose maximal ideal is generated by a weakly regular sequence (`hilbertSamuel_succ_of_isWeaklyRegular`).

## 0.2.8 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/RegularLocus.lean`: the regular locus $\operatorname{Reg}(A)$ of a Noetherian ring $A$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A$. The locus is stable under generization (`regularLocus_stableUnderGeneralization`). The criterion of Nagata ([Mat89, Section 24]): if for each $\mathfrak{p} \in \operatorname{Reg}(A)$ the set $\operatorname{Reg}(A/\mathfrak{p})$ contains a nonempty open set, then $\operatorname{Reg}(A)$ is open (`isOpen_regularLocus_of_exists_isOpen`). ## 0.2.7 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/RegularLocalization.lean`: let $R$ be a regular local ring and let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal. Then $R_\mathfrak{p}$ is a regular local ring (`isRegularLocalRing_localization`). The proof shows $\operatorname{pd} \kappa(\mathfrak{p}) < \infty$ over $R_\mathfrak{p}$ with the module $(R/\mathfrak{p})_\mathfrak{p} \cong \kappa(\mathfrak{p})$. The file also states the characterization of Serre in both directions (`isRegularLocalRing_iff_exists_hasProjectiveDimensionLT`).
- `Singularity/Algebra/SerreConverse.lean`: the converse of the theorem of Serre. Let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring with residue field $k$. If $\operatorname{pd} k < \infty$, then $R$ is regular (`isRegularLocalRing_of_hasProjectiveDimensionLT`). The proof uses prime avoidance with the associated primes, the change of rings $\operatorname{pd}_{R/(x)} (M/xM) \le \operatorname{pd}_R M$ for a nonzerodivisor $x$ on $R$ and $M$, and the fact that $k$ is a direct summand of $\mathfrak{m}/x\mathfrak{m}$ for $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$.
- `Singularity/Algebra/SerreConverse.lean`: let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring. If some $s \ne 0$ has $\mathfrak{m} s = 0$, then each finite module of finite projective dimension is free (`free_of_hasProjectiveDimensionLT_of_socle`). If also $\operatorname{pd} k < \infty$, then $\mathfrak{m} = 0$ (`maximalIdeal_eq_bot_of_socle`). This is the depth zero step of the converse of the theorem of Serre.
- `Singularity/Algebra/GlobalDimension.lean`: over a regular local ring $R$ of dimension $d$, each finite module has projective dimension at most $d$ (`projectiveDimension_le_ringKrullDim`). The proof is by induction on $d$ with the quotient $R/(x)$ for $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$. The file also proves a change of rings: if $x$ is a nonzerodivisor and $N$ is a finite $R/(x)$-module, then $\operatorname{pd}_R N \le \operatorname{pd}_{R/(x)} N + 1$ (`hasProjectiveDimensionLT_of_quotient`).
- `Singularity/Algebra/ExtCriterion.lean`: let $R$ be a Noetherian local ring with residue field $k$, and let $M$ be a finite $R$-module. Then $\operatorname{pd} M \le n$ if and only if $\operatorname{Ext}^{n+1}(M, k) = 0$ (`projectiveDimension_le_iff_subsingleton_ext`). The case $n = 0$ is the statement that $M$ is free if $\operatorname{Ext}^1(M, k) = 0$. Mathlib has the criterion with all finite test modules. This file needs only the test module $k$.
- The workflow `trust` has a new job `lean4lean`. It checks each declaration of the package Singularity again with lean4lean, a type checker for Lean written in Lean. The job checks each module and takes its imports as correct, so it does not check Mathlib again. lean4lean follows the design of the C++ kernel, so this is a second implementation but not an independent design.

## 0.2.6 - 2026-11-24

### Added

- `Singularity/Algebra/HilbertSamuel.lean`: the Hilbert-Samuel function `hilbertSamuel I M n`, the length of $M/I^n M$ in $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\infty\}$. The file proves its value at 0, the recursion $\chi(n+1) = \chi(n) + \ell(I^n M/I^{n+1} M)$, the monotony and the comparison of two ideals. If $R$ is Noetherian, $M$ is finite and $I$ contains a power of a maximal ideal, then each value is finite (`hilbertSamuel_ne_top`). The file also defines the order `order I f` of an element and proves its laws: the order of a sum and of a product, and the intersection theorem of Krull in the form "only 0 has order infinity" for a Noetherian local ring.

With the Mathlib theorem on the quotient by a regular sequence, the residue field has projective dimension $d$ (`projectiveDimension_residueField`). This is the first half of the theorem of Serre.

## 0.2.5 - 2026-11-24
